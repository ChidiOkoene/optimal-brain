# Stage 2: Shape

Turn the pile into one argument. Lead with the answer; make everything below it earn its place.

## Outline.md format

```markdown
# Outline: <Article Title>

## Opening (SCQA)
- Situation: <what the reader already accepts> (F03)
- Complication: <what breaks it> (F07, F08)
- Question: <the question that raises>
- Answer: <governing thought> (C1)

## Governing thought
C1: <one arguable sentence>

## Key points
1. C2: <full-sentence claim> — F07, F12
2. C3: <full-sentence claim> — F08, F10
3. C4: <full-sentence claim, addresses F09> — F09, F14

## Ending
<how the piece lands: return to the Question, one next step for the reader>
```

`C` IDs are rows in the Claim Ledger; `F` IDs are fragments.

## Rules

- **Governing thought is a claim, not a topic.** "Agent memory" is a topic. "Coding agents forget because nothing writes their learning to disk; a linked notes vault fixes that" is a claim.
- **Key points are claims too.** Headings in the final article should read as the claim, not a label.
- **Group by kind, no overlap.** Each key point covers a distinct part of the support. If two overlap, merge them. If one doesn't support the governing thought, cut it or move it to a "Further" section.
- **2 to 5 key points.** One means the governing thought is the key point. Six or more means a missing level of grouping.
- **Order on purpose.** Pick one: logical (cause → effect, problem → fix), chronological, or by importance. Say which in the outline.
- **Counterpoints get a home.** Each `must-address` counterpoint is answered inside a key point, not left for the end.

## Why/how test

Read the outline top-down. After each statement ask "why?" or "how?". The level below must answer it, and nothing below should answer a question nobody asked. Where the chain breaks: add evidence, rewrite the claim to what the evidence supports, or cut.

## Shape vs. strength

If a key point only has `weak` or `exploratory` evidence in the ledger, choose before writing: research more, reframe it as an open question, or cut. Don't let the outline rest on its weakest leg.
